Adapting the Specialisterne Portfolio Workbook to a Bottom-Up Learning Style

The Workbook

What it is

The Specialisterne US Academy Personal Website Workbook walks autistic adult learners through building a one-page IT portfolio website in TeleportHQ over eight weeks. It's designed for people heading into IT support, service desk, and desktop support roles, and it turns coursework and hands-on labs into real work you can show an employer.

I'm honored to have studied in this program. Specialisterne is one of the few places that builds training around how neurodivergent people actually learn and work. Taking its materials apart and studying them closely taught me as much as the course itself.

The seven sections

  1. Hero Banner (#hero)
  2. Professional Story (#story)
  3. Experience & Education (#experience)
  4. Skills & Training (#skills)
  5. IT Problem-Solving Project (#project)
  6. Work Style & Values (#values)
  7. Contact / Connect (#contact)

How I adapted it to the way I learn

The workbook leaves a lot of room for flexibility and creativity, and that works well for many people. I learn bottom-up: I have to see the finished thing before I can build toward it. So I adjusted the process to fit how I think:

  • Layout first. The workbook introduces the full site layout in Week 5. I moved it to Day 1 and set up all seven sections empty, with placeholder text like [Insert 2–3 sentences about your past here], so every piece I wrote had a place to go.
  • A real diagnostic case study. I turned Section 5 into a step-by-step troubleshooting story: the symptom in plain language, at least one cause I ruled out, and a measured result (for example, boot time cut from 6m 10s to 50s).
  • Tight design limits. One scrolling page, no animations, one theme (light or dark), six colors with readable contrast (4.5:1 or better), and no more than two Google Fonts. That kept the AI-assisted builds in TeleportHQ from breaking.
  • Short check-ins. Instead of long updates, I tracked progress with three quick questions: Which section did I fill? What pattern did I use? What's blocking me?

What it did for my portfolio

The program gave me the foundation. Adapting it showed me how I learn best. The result is a portfolio that shows employers how I diagnose problems and keep technical work disciplined, plus a method I can reuse any time I need to learn a new system.

Case Study

Problem

The workbook follows a flexible, top-down structure and introduces the full layout in Week 5. I'm a bottom-up learner. I needed to see the end result first before I could write content that fit it.

Approach

I flipped the order. On Day 1, I built all seven empty sections in TeleportHQ so I could see the whole site before writing a word. I rebuilt the IT case study around a clear troubleshooting pattern that requires a ruled-out dead end and a measured result. And I set firm design limits (one page, no animations, one theme) so the build stayed stable.

Outcome

I built this for myself first. I needed to see the finished site before I could write anything for it. Then I tested the structure by mocking it up in HTML and an AI playground, adjusting until the top-down layout and the bottom-up drafting both worked. What I took from it: garbage in, garbage out. Fix the inputs, and the output takes care of itself.

Demonstrates

Organizing information so it's easy to use, writing clear documentation, and improving training materials based on evidence.
