Case Study · Field Diagnostics

Fix the System, Not the Part: Ending Repeat Overheating on Water-Cooled Welders

Problem

Every Texas summer, service calls came in for water-cooled welders overheating. The standard fix was a new coolant pump or welding cable.

Why It Mattered

Each call meant downtime, and customers were paying for $1,500 pumps and $3,000 cables that often didn't solve it.

Initial View

The common belief was that the pump was underpowered. My hypothesis: something in the system was robbing the pump of its output.

Troubleshooting

  1. Noticed the pumps were chattering, the sound of a pump fighting resistance, which others had missed.
  2. Isolated sections of the cooling loop and measured the flow rate in each.
  3. Used the welder's own data to calculate where the restriction was greatest.

Design finding: The pump met spec on paper but had no margin for field conditions. Ordinary wear pushed it past its limit. Pre-market field testing would have shown the need for a higher-capacity pump.

The Cooling Loop

Where the pump's output was being lost. Orange marks the points that add resistance.

  1. PumpSmall diaphragm pump
  2. HosesTwist and kink with useAdds resistance
  3. Height & AirLift to the torch, trapped airAdds resistance
  4. TorchNeeds steady coolant flow

↻ Coolant returns to the pump. Worn coolant cools less on every pass.

Root Causes

  • Hoses twisting and kinking from handling, the way a power cord coils when it's always twisted in one direction
  • Height difference between the pump and the torch
  • Air trapped in the lines
  • Worn-out coolant that had lost its cooling capacity

Fix & Verification

Untwisted or rerouted hoses, purged lines with shop air and replaced old coolant. Then I taught the shop technicians what was really causing the overheating and the limits of their equipment, so they could prevent it themselves instead of waiting for a service call.

Role of AI

Later used AI to explore a lower-resistance cooling design concept.

Lesson

You don't treat a smoker's cough with cough syrup. You get them to stop smoking. Find the real cause. Testing a product under real-world conditions before launch catches what a spec sheet can't.

Training Design · Specialisterne USA Academy

A Bottom-Up Path Through the Portfolio Course

Problem

The course built the portfolio site section by section and showed the finished page only near the end. For learners who need to see the whole structure first, that made it hard to know how the parts fit.

What I Did

Mapped the course workbook into a structure I could see whole, then wrote an opt-in second path: deploy seven empty, labeled sections on day one and fill them as lessons arrive.

What It Includes

A manual fallback if the AI builder fails, two quality gates for case studies, a three-question Friday check-in, and the same graduation standards as the main path.
